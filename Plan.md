“And they continued steadfastly in the apostles’ doctrine and fellowship, in the breaking of bread, and in prayers” (Acts‬ ‭2‬:‭42‬ ‭NKJV‬‬). As it was in the early church, so it is now. The Lutheran church has held staunchly to the apostles’ doctrine; look no further than our dogmatic and confessional writings to see that we have held to this same principle. Likewise, our hymnody and liturgy testify of our rich fellowship; for age upon age the Church of the Augsburg Confession has sung in unity the greatest anthems ever composed in Christendom. At the center of our worship is the breaking of the bread, the Mass. Here we receive the body and blood of Christ under the bread and wine, the most profound of mysteries. Finally, we retain the ancient customs of the Church, along with her readings, collects, psalmist, and the like. 

The main book for the celebration of the breaking of the bread is the missal, for which even now is being developed from ancient sources for modern usage in the Lutheran church. However, the use of the breviary, the book for the daily prayers of the church, has been mostly lost to our people, except for a few attempts (Treasury of Daily Prayer, The Brotherhood Prayer Book). For that reason  am endeavoring on producing a breviary for the Lutheran church. 

At its core, for this was its ancient purpose, the breviary will recite all 150 psalms in a week and it will read all of the Bible in one year with appropriate lessons from the Fathers. From this basic intention all else will be derived.
